## What you will make

Build a finger-controlled memory game with a BBC micro:bit and switches made from everyday materials.

The micro:bit shows a sequence of numbers from `1` to `3`. Tap the matching finger contacts against your thumb contact in the same order. Every successful round adds another number. Repeat five terms correctly to complete the reboot.

### You will need:

- A BBC micro:bit V2
- A USB data cable
- A battery pack and two AAA batteries
- Four crocodile or alligator clip leads
- A band or strap for holding the micro:bit on your wrist
- Conductive tape
- Scrap paper or thin card
- Ordinary tape
- Scissors

The project does not use the speaker or microphone.

### How the controller works

You will make four loose paper bands. Conductive tape on each band creates a contact:

- Thumb: `GND`
- Index finger: pin `P0`, which enters `1`
- Middle finger: pin `P1`, which enters `2`
- Ring finger: pin `P2`, which enters `3`

A simple band or strap holds the micro:bit on your wrist. Touching a numbered finger contact to the thumb contact closes that circuit. Your hand becomes the missing control panel.

> [!IMPORTANT]
>
> Make every paper band loose enough to remove immediately. Attach conductive tape and crocodile clips to paper tabs, not to your skin. Never connect the `3V` and `GND` rings together.
